# Learning Levels

Present one level as a required structured choice before starting research,
even when the request appears to imply a level. Generate only that version.

## Primary

Use everyday observations, concrete nouns, short causal sentences, and familiar
comparisons. Avoid formulas, unexplained field terminology, and detailed
thermodynamic notation. Advanced processes may be described by their observable
effect and marked as a deeper boundary.

Draw analogies from toys, food, weather, traffic, simple tools, and recognizable
body experiences. Keep each compared relationship directly visible. The
analogy's limit should correct the most likely literal reading in one plain
sentence.

## Middle

Introduce the standard school concepts needed to explain the chain: energy
conversion, current, voltage, force, simple formulas, and controlled
experiments. Define terms before using them and keep mathematics secondary to the
causal explanation.

Draw analogies from school, sports, plumbing, household devices, and controlled
experiments. A single water, road, crowd, or queue scene may extend across
adjacent nodes when its mapping remains accurate. State which familiar element
maps to force, energy, current, voltage, or another abstract quantity.

## High

Use precise subject terminology and quantitative relationships when they improve
the explanation: electric field, potential difference, drift velocity,
electrochemical potential, energy conservation, and model boundaries.

Use familiar systems that can carry quantitative or relational structure, such
as pumps and pressure, springs, maps, slopes, or constrained flows. The analogy
may be more precise, but it must still name the point at which the model becomes
misleading instead of implying that the real system behaves literally like the
familiar object.

## Stage consistency

- The causal chain, node IDs, visuals, and videos remain the same for one page.
- Adapt questions, labels, answers, summaries, core knowledge, facts, tags, and
  visual captions, analogies, and analogy limits to the chosen level.
- Choose familiar reference experiences the selected stage can reasonably know.
  Do not use an advanced analogy that requires a second unfamiliar explanation.
- Do not hide a causal step merely because the language must be simpler.
- Do not include content for unselected levels in JSON or HTML.
- If the user asks for another level later, generate a separate page.
